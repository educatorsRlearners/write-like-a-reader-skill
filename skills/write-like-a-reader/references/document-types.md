# Document types

The questions below arise in the order shown. Use the general list for any expository text, and the type-specific list when the document fits one.

## General (any expository text)

- A claim: How does that work, and why should I believe it?
- An abstraction: Can you give an example?
- A comparison: Compared to what?
- A finding: So what?
- A recommendation: What do I do next?
- A term the reader may not share: What does it mean here?

Ask no question whose answer is a plain fact. Ask none that the sentence has already answered.

## Blog post or essay

1. Why is this worth my time?
2. What is the claim?
3. Why should I believe it (evidence, example)?
4. What objection comes to mind, and is it answered?
5. What changes for me?

## Article or explainer

1. What is this about?
2. Why now?
3. How does it work?
4. What is the one thing to remember?

## Report or proposal

1. What is the finding or ask?
2. What supports it?
3. What are the options and costs?
4. What should I decide, and by when?

## Tutorial or guide

1. What will I be able to do?
2. What do I need first?
3. What is step one?
4. How do I know it worked?
5. What if it fails?

## Documentation or README

1. What is this?
2. Why should I care?
3. Will it work for me?
4. How do I install it?
5. How do I use it (a runnable example)?
6. Where do I go for more?

## Design doc

1. What problem is this solving?
2. What was considered and rejected?
3. What is the decision, and why?
4. What are the risks?

## Long email or announcement

1. What happened or is changing?
2. Why does it affect me?
3. What do I need to do, and by when?
